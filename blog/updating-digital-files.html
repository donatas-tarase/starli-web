WIX NOTES / DIGITAL PRODUCTS

A practical guide to updating digital files after a sale

Written November 3, 2026 · Starli Labs

Illustrative app view.

You sold a digital guide, then spotted an outdated link. Or perhaps your template has improved and you want future customers to receive a newer edition. The file is easy to edit; deciding who should receive the update is the important part.

Quick answer: Before replacing a digital file, decide whether the change is for future orders only or whether eligible previous buyers should receive access too. Saving an updated version, granting access to earlier buyers and notifying them are distinct decisions. Check your app’s current plan and settings before promising an update.

When should you update a digital product?

Some changes correct an error: a broken link, a typo that changes an instruction or a file that will not open. Others add value, such as a new lesson, fresh examples or another file format. A larger redesign might be a new product rather than an update included in an earlier purchase.

Start by writing down what changed and why. For a typo in a decorative cover, a future-orders update may be sufficient. If a workbook has a calculation error that affects customers’ results, you may decide to offer a corrected version to previous buyers. These are business decisions: consider your purchase terms and the expectations you set when selling.

Prepare the new files

  1. Keep an untouched copy of the previous version. You may need to compare it with the update or answer a customer’s question about an earlier purchase.
  2. Test the actual download. Open each file, follow its instructions and verify links, formulas or included assets. If you distribute a ZIP, extract it on another device as a buyer would.
  3. Use a clear filename and release note. A name such as meal-planner-v2.pdf is easier to distinguish from the earlier file than final-final.pdf. Summarize meaningful changes in plain language.
  4. Check compatibility. If an editable template now requires different software or features, say so on the product page and in customer communication.

If your package includes several files, verify the whole set. Updating a guide while leaving an older companion worksheet inside the bundle can make the customer’s experience more confusing than the original issue.

Choose who gets the update

Future purchases only

This approach fits a change you want to include in new sales without changing what earlier customers received. Check your product description and terms so the current offer reflects the file being sold. In Digital Downloads, changing product defaults does not silently rewrite previous purchase snapshots.

Eligible previous buyers as well

You might choose this for a correction or an update included in your offer. Before granting access, check which product and variant the update belongs to, who is eligible and what access rules will apply. Do not assume that an earlier purchase automatically receives the new file just because a version was saved.

Digital Downloads supports file version history and previous-buyer updates on eligible plans. The current Enterprise plan includes versioning, and some older plans may also include it; check the current plan details and the controls shown in your account. If a previous-buyer grant is available, review the proposed audience and resulting access before applying it.

Treat access and notification as separate decisions

Giving a customer access and sending an email are not the same event. Before telling buyers they have a new version, confirm that access has been granted to the intended orders and review the notification settings. A saved file or version alone is not proof that an email was sent or that a customer received it.

A useful update email answers four questions: what changed, who it affects, how to reach the file and where to ask for help. For example:

Subject: An update to your planning workbook
We corrected the annual total on the budget sheet. The updated workbook is available through your download link. Need help opening it? Reply using the support contact on your order.

Use wording like this only after confirming that the recipient has access and that the described contact route works. When sending at scale, review recipients and settings carefully; a record that an email was accepted by a sending service is not proof it reached an inbox or was opened.

A practical release checklist

  • Confirm the product, variant, filenames and complete replacement bundle.
  • Read the product’s update promise and select the intended audience.
  • Check plan availability, access rules and which previous purchases are eligible.
  • Test the new files and the buyer-facing download experience.
  • If you intend to update previous buyers, review and apply the appropriate access grant; verify the outcome.
  • If you intend to notify buyers, review the email and delivery settings separately and verify the outcome.
  • Keep a record of what changed, when and who was meant to receive it.

Frequently asked questions

Does replacing a file automatically change every previous purchase?

Do not assume that it does. Digital Downloads preserves previous purchase snapshots, and updating earlier buyers requires an explicit eligible workflow. Check what the app shows before making a promise to customers.

Can I give previous buyers access without emailing them?

Access and notification are separate operations in the app’s version workflow. Availability and the exact controls depend on your plan and configuration. Review the selected action before applying it.

Should every improvement be free for previous buyers?

That depends on what you promised and the nature of the change. Decide whether the update is a correction, an included improvement or a separate product, and make the terms easy to understand.
