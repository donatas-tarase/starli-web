How to make digital delivery emails clear and useful

Written November 3, 2026 · Starli Labs

Illustrative app view.

Your customer has paid. The next email should make one thing obvious: how to get what they bought. A delivery message can be brief and still answer the questions that matter, from finding the files to getting help when something does not work.

Quick answer: A useful digital delivery email identifies the purchase, gives a clear route to the files, explains any relevant access terms and offers a support contact. Test the message and link as a customer before launch. Available email customization depends on the service and plan you use.

What a delivery email needs to do

A delivery email is a practical part of the purchase experience. The buyer may read it on a phone, in a crowded inbox or several days after checkout. They should recognize your business, understand why they received the message and find the next step without searching through a long introduction.

Before designing a template, write down the customer’s path: a paid order creates a delivery, a message directs the buyer to their files, and a support route handles exceptions. Check that each step is actually configured in your store. A good email cannot compensate for an incorrect product-to-file connection.

Six details to include

1. A recognizable sender and subject

Use a sender identity your buyer can associate with your store and a subject that states the purpose. “Your download is ready” or “Your Studio North files are ready” tells the reader more than “Welcome!” Make sure your actual sending setup supports the identity you choose.

2. A short confirmation

Thank the customer and identify the purchase or order where your template supports it. A simple opening such as “Thanks for ordering the Creative Studio Kit” reassures the reader that the email relates to the right transaction. Do not insert personal details you do not need.

3. One clear action

Use a descriptive link or button such as “Open your downloads.” Place it where it is easy to find, and make surrounding text clear enough that the customer understands where the action leads. Check the destination and test the full flow; a button in a design mockup is not the same thing as a working link.

4. Useful instructions

If a bundle needs to be unzipped, a template requires particular software or a file is intended for printing, include a short instruction or link to a getting-started page. Put longer troubleshooting steps elsewhere so the download action remains easy to find.

5. Accurate access terms

If a download limit or expiry applies, tell buyers in language that matches the rules configured for their purchase. For example, “Your link is available for 14 days” is helpful only when 14 days is the rule that applies. Put important terms on the product page too, so customers can see them before buying.

6. A way to get help

Give customers a support email address or another contact route that your business monitors. Say what information will help you locate the order, but avoid asking people to send payment card details, passwords or the purchased file itself.

A simple example to adapt

Subject: Your Creative Studio Kit is ready

Thanks for your order! Your files are ready to download.

Open your downloads

You’ll find your files and any applicable access information on the download page. Need a hand? Contact us at [your support email].

This is sample copy, not a functioning template. Replace the bracketed details, add your real link through the system you use, and check that the language matches your product and actual access settings.

Add your brand without hiding the action

Your business name, familiar colors and a short, human tone can make the email feel connected to the rest of your store. Keep contrast strong enough for the text and button to be readable. A logo and decorative images are optional; the essential information should still make sense if images do not load.

Digital Downloads provides delivery email tools, with customization depending on the plan. The current Enterprise plan includes custom email design; some older plans may also include it. Confirm the options in your account and the current plan details before promising a particular look or automation.

Test the complete buyer journey

  1. Confirm the product setup. Check the files linked to each product or variant and that your purchase terms match the configured access rules.
  2. Follow a test order. Use a suitable test purchase and verify when the order is treated as paid in your setup.
  3. Find the message. Check the subject, sender and recipient. Look at the email on both a phone and a larger screen.
  4. Use the link. Open the download page, download the expected files and check that the access information is understandable.
  5. Try the support route. Make sure the contact address or page is correct and monitored.

Wix Stores also has a native digital delivery flow. Its digital product guide explains when Wix sends links after immediate and delayed payments. The timing and wording of messages in an app may differ, so test the combination you actually use.

When the buyer cannot find the email

First verify that the order is paid and check which email address was used. Ask the buyer to check their inbox and spam folder. Review the order and delivery activity available in your setup, then use the appropriate action for the situation.

Resending an email does not by itself extend an expired link, restore revoked access or reset the download count. Check the access state first; if a change is needed, use the controls available on your plan and confirm what you actually changed before replying.

Frequently asked questions

Does my delivery email need a custom design?

No. A clear subject, working link and support route matter more than decoration. Add branding when it improves recognition and is available in your setup.

Should I include the file directly as an attachment?

Choose the delivery method supported by your store and suited to the file. A link-based flow can present access information and the purchased files on a download page; test the experience and describe it accurately to customers.

Does an email marked as sent mean the customer opened it?

No. A sending status is not proof that a message reached the inbox, was opened or led to a completed download. Review the specific activity your delivery system reports and avoid assuming more than it shows.
