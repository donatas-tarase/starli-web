WIX NOTES / DIGITAL PRODUCTS

What digital products can you sell on your Wix store?

Written November 3, 2026 · Starli Labs

Illustrative app view.

A useful digital product does not have to start as a hundred-page course. A simple checklist, a set of templates or a short guide can be valuable when it helps a particular person complete a particular task. The question is not just “What can I upload?” but “What will someone be glad to use?”

Quick answer: Wix sellers can offer digital files such as ebooks, PDFs, templates, presets, audio and ZIP bundles. Choose an idea that fits your expertise, can be described clearly and is practical for your customer to use. Check your chosen delivery setup and file limits before promising a format or experience.

Digital product ideas to explore

Guides and ebooks

A guide can turn a question you answer repeatedly into a structured resource. A wedding photographer might create a preparation guide; a language teacher might offer a practice workbook; a consultant might package an onboarding checklist. Explain the outcome and the intended reader, then show a sample page so customers can judge the level of detail.

Templates and planners

Templates give people a starting point. Consider a budget spreadsheet, social media planning sheet, lesson planner, contract intake form or printable habit tracker. State which software or device the customer needs and whether they can edit the file. A visually appealing template is less useful if a buyer cannot open it.

Creative assets and presets

Designers and photographers can package icons, textures, brushes, presentation layouts or editing presets. Include compatible software and version requirements, and explain whether the buyer receives a personal or commercial licence. If you include third-party assets, check that your licence permits redistribution in the form you intend to sell.

Audio and other downloadable media

Musicians, educators and wellness creators might sell sound effects, backing tracks, spoken lessons or guided recordings. Name the file format, duration and intended use. A short preview can help buyers decide whether the sound and content fit their needs. Selling audio files does not necessarily mean offering a streaming or membership service; describe exactly what is included.

Bundles that solve one larger problem

A bundle could combine a PDF guide, a worksheet and a set of editable templates. The value comes from how the pieces work together. List each included file, the supported applications and the access terms. For example, a “launch kit” might include a launch checklist, three email prompts and a planning sheet—not an unspecified collection of bonus files.

How to choose your first product

Start with a problem you understand. Look at recurring questions from your customers, common points of confusion in your process, or a task you have already made easier for yourself. Then narrow the audience: “a pricing worksheet for freelance photographers” is easier to explain than “a business spreadsheet for everyone.”

Before building a large catalog, outline one useful result and the smallest set of files needed to achieve it. Write a sample product description before you finish the product. If it is difficult to explain who the file helps or what is inside, refine the idea. Ask potential customers for feedback on a sample; do not treat interest from a few conversations as proof of sales.

Choose a format customers can actually use

Choose the format based on the job. PDFs suit fixed-layout guides and printables; editable documents or spreadsheets suit worksheets; ZIP files can group several related files. If a customer needs an account, a paid application or a particular device to use an item, say so before checkout. Test the file on a second device or ask someone unfamiliar with the product to open it.

Wix’s own digital product guide describes its supported formats and file limits. If you use a delivery app, check its current storage limits and supported workflow as well. Do not assume that a file limit or supported format is the same across every plan.

Make the product page answer the important questions

  • What is included? Name the files, format and quantity. Show a preview where useful.
  • Who is it for? Describe the problem and the experience level it suits.
  • What is required? List compatible software, devices, printing needs and any separate account or subscription.
  • What happens after payment? Explain how delivery works and where the customer can get help.
  • What are the usage terms? State relevant licence, download-access and update terms accurately.

Price is a business decision, not a formula hidden inside a file format. Consider your production and support time, the value of the problem solved and the alternatives your customer can reasonably compare. Avoid promising future updates unless you have decided how you will deliver them.

Plan the delivery before launch

Wix Stores already sends digital products after payment. An app such as Digital Downloads can add a place to connect files to products or variants and manage access after a sale. Custom download rules and access-management features depend on the plan. Check the current plan details and test your own workflow before publishing your terms.

Walk through the order yourself: read the product page, complete a test purchase, find the delivery email, open the file and try the first task a buyer will do. If something is confusing, update the product or instructions before sending traffic to it.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell more than one file with a product?

There are several possible approaches. Wix describes grouping files into a ZIP for its native digital product flow. Digital Downloads can connect files to products and variants; check the current plan and your setup for applicable limits.

Do digital products need ongoing support?

Often they need some. Buyers may need help opening a file, understanding compatibility or finding a delivery email. Make a contact route clear and leave time to maintain the information customers rely on.

Should I sell individual files or a bundle?

Start with the customer’s task. A single file is easier to understand when it solves one focused problem; a bundle helps when related items support a coherent outcome. In both cases, make the contents clear before purchase.
